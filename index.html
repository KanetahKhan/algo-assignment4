THE THREE CORE ALGORITHMS

From a road network to a feasible plan.

  1. BFS checks reachability. Closed roads are removed. A traversal identifies which locations remain reachable from each residential area.
  2. Dijkstra finds a fastest route. Road travel time is the nonnegative edge weight. This route assumes no queue and does not reserve capacity.
  3. Edmonds–Karp allocates people over time. A copy of every location is created for each minute. Road edges advance time; waiting edges allow queues. Max-flow finds the maximum number who arrive by the deadline.
  4. Binary search finds the earliest full evacuation. The solver searches deadlines up to 60 minutes. More time never reduces the number who can arrive.
MODEL ASSUMPTIONS

What the numbers mean.

  • Road capacity is people entering per minute, per direction. A two-way road has independent capacity in each direction.
  • Travel takes a fixed, whole number of minutes. Departures begin at minute 0. Arrival exactly at the deadline counts.
  • Shelter capacity is total space, shared across all arrival times. It does not reset every minute.
  • People may wait at locations. Waiting space is unlimited; no road-occupancy, panic, hazard-spread, or intersection-capacity model is included.
  • Edits create a new plan starting at minute 0. Playback visualizes that plan; it does not model a mid-journey aftershock.
  • The objective is maximum arrivals by a deadline. It does not optimize fairness or total travel time among equally good plans.
